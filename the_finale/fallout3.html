The Capital Wasteland

Despite being the 3rd game in the Fallout series, Fallout 3 was the first developed in a first to 3rd person style. This design choice opened the game up to a much wider audience, and subsequently devoured countless hours of my adolescence. For anyone who enjoys exploring and hunting for hidden secrets, this game cannot be ignored. Featuring 225 marked locations spread across approximately 8.4 square miles, this map is massive. Every location is unique, hiding special items, bits of lore, and expertly crafted environmental story telling. Map size isn't everything though, there are also around 319 named characters featuring over 40,000 lines of dialog truly bringing this wasteland to life!

Time however, hasn't been kind to this open world gem. While I have fond nostalgic memories of the capitol wasteland, the game is riddled with bugs that Bethesda never bothered to fix. Occasional frustrations aside it's more than playable if you can withstand clunky movement and combat mechanics. Although if you decide to step back in time for this one, I highly recommend using some mods to improve the experience. One of the best options is Tale of Two Wastelands.

Rivet City

A disheveled aircraft carrier dry-docked on the Potomac River, equipped with a retractable bridge for security. Other than a well stocked vault, this is hands down one of the safest places to call home in the wasteland. Rivet City is home to a thriving market, restaurants of questionable quality, and dozens of characters willing to send you on a quest! Despite its awesome atmosphere, this in game city has several sections requiring a loading screen to access; thus it can be frustrating at times.

Just Wandering

If this website is an attempt to lead more gamers to Fallout 3, perhaps I haven't glazed this title quite enough. That's because I know this one isn't for everyone. However, if you like building out a unique character and turning every stone in an open world grab a copy. Fallout 3 isn't quite as adrenaline inducing as other options, but the atmosphere, environmental story telling, and wacky shenanigans are second to none. So if I've peaked your interest, turn up your pipboy radio and sew some more chaos in the Capitol Wasteland.
